A research brief is written from the owner's question, not from a working hypothesis: the categories, candidate answers or framing an issue or a conversation has collected so far stay out of the brief. The hypothesis comes out of the research findings, the same way system design comes from the domain rather than the other way round. A brief that carries the hypothesis (e.g. "these three dimensions, and any others") gets it echoed back as findings, and the research can no longer tell you whether the framing was right; delegated researchers, who see only the brief, are especially prone to this. The Guide says domain claims are grounded in sources first, but not that the *question put to the sources* must be free of the design's own framing.
