Projects whose aim is a business rather than a system (a venture: possibly software, possibly physical goods) share approach that no Guide holds, because the Map's Archetypes are shapes of systems. What surfaced in two ventures' strategy work:

- A strategy is worked as Rumelt's kernel (diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent actions). The goal sets the challenge, the diagnosis poses what makes it hard as a series of challenges, and the crux is the most important one that can be addressed.
- When the exit is the goal, it shapes the structure from the outset (entity, equity, data and accounts a buyer can take over), and it can decide the vehicle (an exit for a small team points to software). The team size is staged: one person proves the concept, then a team in the low tens scales it to a sale. The one sourced comparable had about 30 staff when it sold.
- "What good looks like" is benchmarked against peers of the venture's size, not the top of a long-tailed market.
- "Meaningful money" is settled by naming the market the exit sells into, and the measure that market prices (small studios sell on earnings, in distinct markets by size), not by a price target, which would need a financial model.
- A proof stage means nothing until its audience is named: proof is whatever convinces whoever funds the next stage. A self-funded venture's proof answers the owner's own question ("is it safe to spend on growth?"), which fixed what counted as proof in one grill.
- The owner rejected a stopping rule (a budget of attempts, then revisit). The strategy is written to work, and what redirects it is refuted hypotheses revising the kernel, not a spent budget. This fits the Project knowledge Guide's end-state Stance, if a venture's kernel is treated as its system design.
- Repo and tooling setup comes after the plan, not before: what is shared, what is separate and what a buyer takes over decide the repo's shape.

How it would apply: a Guide with Scope any Stack × a "ventures" Archetype (stretching "shape of a system", which the Promoter should weigh), written only once these are settled in the ventures and the strategy sources have been read rather than recalled (*Good Strategy Bad Strategy*, *The Crux*, and others).
