A reading rule with per-fixture clauses ("on a fixture where the measure is already at or below the baseline's, …") was declared with the fixtures it covers listed beside it, worked out by hand from the baseline table. The list missed one. The fixture the list missed fell under a different clause by the rule than by the list, and under the other clause the change would have been refuted. The agent judged it by the rule and flagged it. The owner had to confirm afterwards which of the two had been declared, which is the kind of after-the-reading call declaring rules in advance exists to prevent.

How it would apply: a clause whose scope depends on the baseline states the rule alone, and the test or report computes which cases it covers from the baseline. If examples are given, they are labelled as examples, never as the list. Where a hand-made list has gone out with the rule, the rule is what was declared.
