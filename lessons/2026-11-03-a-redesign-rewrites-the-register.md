The end-state Stance says how the end state is revised: a refuted hypothesis changes the system design, and declared values change only with it. It doesn't cover a revision that starts from the concepts rather than from a reading: the owner finds that a core concept of the design is wrong (what a unit of the domain *is*), and the design is redrawn around a new one. Then the hypotheses were declared over concepts that are going away, and holding the redesign to them judges the new design by the old one's terms. During such a redesign the design comes first, and the hypotheses are rewritten to match it, possibly wholesale, as part of the same change; the register guides again once the new design and its hypotheses are declared. What keeps this from becoming an excuse for Drift is that the trigger is a change of concept, written into the design as one, never a reading that came out badly.
