The categories an act's schema offers (an enum of roles, kinds or statuses) are domain claims, and they need sources like any other. A news-extraction app's design gave the participants in a statement four roles invented from recall, and two acts' instructions and schemas were built on them. Two rounds of rewording one act's instructions each moved one count and disturbed another. The cause sat higher up. The glossary named one relation twice (as "involves" and as a kind of "connection"), so two acts judged it. No term existed for a simpler relation the design needed. And one role bundled a reliable judgement (what a statement says something of) with the least reliable one the literature knows (what a text is about). Research briefed from the owner's question, with none of the project's terms in it, found the established layering and a sourced pair of roles. The fix was language first, then design, then the acts.

Two things follow:

- **Rewording that doesn't converge is a zoom-out signal.** When successive wording changes to an act trade one failure for another, check the glossary for a term used for two things, or a relation with no term, and check the act's categories for a source, before trying another wording.
- **A category's name is shown to the model and to the owner, so its connotations are part of the definition.** A role named *affected* reads as harm ("those affected by the floods"), and labels people with a judgement the statement doesn't make. Choose the neutral term the sources use, and list the loaded ones under *Avoid* in the glossary.
