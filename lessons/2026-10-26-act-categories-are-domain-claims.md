(The first half of this Lesson, that an act's categories are domain claims needing sources, was promoted to the Project knowledge Guide. This is what is left, for the Model calls Guide.)

A news-extraction app's acts were built on four roles invented from recall. Two rounds of rewording one act's instructions each moved one count and disturbed another. The cause sat higher up. The glossary named one relation twice, so two acts judged it. No term existed for a simpler relation the design needed. And one role bundled a reliable judgement with the least reliable one the literature knows (what a text is about). The fix was language first, then design, then the acts.

- **Rewording that doesn't converge is a zoom-out signal.** When successive wording changes to an act trade one failure for another, check the glossary for a term used for two things, or a relation with no term, and check the act's categories for a source, before trying another wording.
- **A category's name is shown to the model and to the owner, so its connotations are part of the definition.** A role named *affected* reads as harm ("those affected by the floods"), and labels people with a judgement the statement doesn't make. Choose the neutral term the sources use, and list the loaded ones under *Avoid* in the glossary.
