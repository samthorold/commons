The grill Skills suit a design whose concepts are already fixed: they walk the decision tree one branch at a time, each question with a recommended answer, narrowing towards a choice. On an abstract concept, where the concepts themselves are in question and are worked out by moving between them and between projects as examples, that method fails in two ways. A recommended answer with every question anchors: the owner responds to the agent's candidate concept, and the question gets reshaped around it. And splitting the question into branches fixes it before it is right: each answer reformulated the grill instead of working it, the same step-by-step drift the session was about. A session on an abstract concept needs a different shape: the owner thinks aloud, the agent holds the concepts that come up open without recommending, tracks where they conflict, and tests them against concrete cases across projects, converging on decisions only once the concepts have settled. Whether that is a mode of the grill Skills or a Procedure of its own is open.
