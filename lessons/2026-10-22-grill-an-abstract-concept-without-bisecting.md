The grill Skills suit a design whose concepts are already fixed: they walk the decision tree one branch at a time, each question with a recommended answer, narrowing towards a choice. On an abstract concept, where the concepts themselves are in question and are worked out by moving between them and between projects as examples, that method fails. Splitting the question into branches fixes it before it is right, and a confident recommendation per question gets the question reshaped around the agent's candidate: each answer reformulated the grill instead of working it.

A second session on the same concept, run in an open format, worked. Its shape:

- **The owner thinks aloud; the agent holds the concepts that come up**, in the owner's words, in a running list (concepts, tensions, which cases each was tested against, what was dropped and why), and **plays back where the concepts stand** every few exchanges, not after every one. The playback is what kept the session and its output usable.
- **No fixed agenda.** The owner picks where to go next; the agent may suggest, loosely.
- **Light recommendations are fine, even helpful**, where many disparate concepts are in play. The failure isn't recommending; it's a question that invites acceptance without engagement. So each question asked is one that genuinely needs the owner, and questions an agent could answer confidently are answered, not asked.
- **Concepts are tested against real cases across projects, and against the research already done**, read rather than recalled. The cases were produced under the flaws being addressed, so they show where a concept is missing, not what it should be.
- **The agent's attempts get rejected, and that is the work**: a reformulation the owner calls wordplay is dropped and recorded as dropped.
- It ends at a natural pause by folding the list into the issue (working concepts, open items, candidates for further research), not by deciding.

The underlying point sorts questions by confidence. A question the agent could answer confidently doesn't belong in a grill: answer it from the Commons and the repo's docs, record it and carry on. A question it can't answer confidently is what a grill is for. The "for each question, provide your recommended answer" line exists because grills have been full of the first kind; the fix is a Commons rich enough that grills are left with the second. Whether the open format is a mode of the grill Skills or a Procedure of its own is still open.
