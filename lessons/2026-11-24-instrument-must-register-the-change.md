Before a reading of "the change didn't get through" is accepted, check that the instrument can register the change at all. A probe that perturbs an input and reads the response sets a threshold, but if its perturbation falls where the candidate's new description is blind (or where even a correct model would barely respond), the reading is about the instrument, not the hypothesis, and the rule's next step ("build a better description") chases nothing. The check is usually cheap and needs no new model: tabulate what the perturbation actually does on real cases (here, the swapped-in item was unaffordable to the actor it was meant to matter to in all but 0.5% of cases, so no description of affordability could move). Fixing an instrument after a failed reading looks like moving the line, so the fix should rest on a defect shown from the instrument's own definition and the data, not from any candidate's score, and be recorded as such in the design before the re-read.
