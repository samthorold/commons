The Stance says a property that relies on a case appearing names the fixture it needs and asserts the case is present. That is not enough when the property is worded over the whole fixture rather than over the case's own output.

The case: a hand-made fixture holds a reaction to news, and the properties were "no new thread is started for it" and "it is not added to a thread", checked over everything the downstream act did with the fixture's input. Then the upstream extraction act's instructions changed, and it legitimately drew a second item from the same input: the event being reacted to. The downstream act rightly filed that item, and both properties were refuted on every sample, while the reaction itself was handled correctly every time.

The rule that would have held: a property tied to a case names the output it rests on (the extracted item that is the reaction), asserts that output is present, and checks only it. Everything else the input yields is free to vary. Without that, an upstream change refutes a downstream property for doing its job, and the only ways out look like moving the line after a reading. Narrowing the property then needs the owner's say each time, with the evidence that the failure came from a different output.
