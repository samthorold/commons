A venture that buys users and reads what groups of them earn and keep can read those groups from totals (per product, market and day) without holding any data about single users, and should start there. Data per user tends to creep into such designs from the tools, which compute a group's figures as the sum of their users' events. Once per-user data is copied out of the vendor (a raw event export), the venture takes on retention limits, security and every deletion request for that copy, and in the UK and EU only users who consent are counted anyway. Holding only totals keeps per-user data inside the vendor's services under their deletion tools, and leaves a record that can be kept indefinitely and shown to a buyer. Its costs are explicit and accepted up front: groups bought in the same market must not overlap in time, because totals by market and day can't separate them, and unpaid users and users who refuse consent are mixed into the totals. The question to ask early is "what does the reading need?", answered before "what does the tool give?".
