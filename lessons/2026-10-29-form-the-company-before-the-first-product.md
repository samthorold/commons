A venture whose exit is the sale of a company should form the company before its first product ships, rather than start in the owner's personal accounts and move later. The platforms make the move costly, not merely slow. An ad network's account type can be fixed for good (individual or organisation), with no documented way to move apps between accounts, so a move means a new account and new IDs shipped in an update to every product. A store's app transfer can leave the earnings reports and orders behind. Each analytics link has to be redone by hand, and a change of data controller raises whether users' consent carries over. A company's set-up costs and lead times (yearly filings, a D-U-N-S number taking weeks) are better treated as part of the cost of the attempt, paid while the first product is built. The design can then be written against a role (the publisher) rather than a legal form, and the company's own set-up kept as a separate track. This extends the inbox Lesson `ventures-need-a-guide` ("the exit shapes the structure from the outset").
