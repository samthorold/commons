In a model whose findings emerge from a complex substrate (an insurance market's loss and settlement engine, an ecology), the substrate is itself a part of the end state, with hypotheses that have to hold before any finding built on it counts. Some of those hypotheses can be stated exactly (a claim never exceeds its sum insured). Others can only be stated as a direction: an insurer's aggregate attritional spread falls as its pool grows while its catastrophe spread doesn't, or a population recovers after a shock rather than collapsing. "Sensible" for a substrate is often only definable that way.

So the substrate's hypotheses take the same reading rules as the findings', including the directional template (most seeds agree on the sign, the mean's interval excludes zero; refuted when the interval lies below the panel's measured sensitivity). The exact ones run on every change. The substrate gates every finding as a design principle, and a change to it makes every hypothesis Due.

Phenomena are often read in controlled variants of the reference setup, with one thing changed and all else the same, to isolate an effect. A hypothesis names which setup it's read in. Requiring every phenomenon to appear in one reference setup is the wrong test.

Expected to recur in the other agent-based and ecological models (explorers' ecology).
