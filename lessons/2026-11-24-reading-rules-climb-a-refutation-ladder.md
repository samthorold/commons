A hypothesis whose only test is one coarse, expensive reading (hours of paired simulation runs, pass or fail on the whole phenomenon) turns each refutation into a diagnosis and then another full reading. Over many cycles the project iterates at the slowest possible rate, and most failures turn out to have been visible within the first few percent of a run, or in arithmetic on one actor. The end-state detail says cheap coarse tests near the top can refute but not confirm. It doesn't say that a reading rule should be built that way: declare rungs ordered by cost (exact arithmetic, then actor-level rules on a few agents, then an early window of one run, then the full panel with a sequential stop and an in-run abort), each a refute-only necessary condition, the next run only once the cheaper ones hold, and only the top rung able to support. A rung that fails gets a fix designed against that rung. The register can record the highest rung passed.
