A reading rule's threshold needs its own stated derivation (what a correct system would read, or what change would matter), written beside it. Thresholds written in one pass when hypotheses first get reading rules tend to be round numbers chosen against whatever the instrument then showed, and later readings then treat them as load-bearing: experiments are judged and steps ordered by a line nobody can justify. When declaring or reviewing a hypothesis, ask for the threshold's derivation as well as the hypothesis's source; one without a derivation is provisional and should say so.
