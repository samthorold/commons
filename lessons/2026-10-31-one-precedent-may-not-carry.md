One piece of feedback shown as a precedent may not change a small model's judgement, even on the very case it was given on. On a small model, with the precedent shown as data after the input, two acts held 5 of 5 on the same case. A third, a reading act asked again about a reference the owner had marked wrong, still repeated it in 2 of 5 (5 of 5 without the precedent). In single-sample probes, no act carried a precedent to a case that was only similar.

The Guide's *a precedent is a case, not a rule* says how to show precedents, but not that their effect is uncertain. Treat whether a precedent carries, to the same case and above all to similar ones, as a hypothesis: record each act's precedent case on the case the feedback was given on first, and read carrying by analogy in use rather than assume it.
