A run of an emergent system costs what its state grows to, not what its rules cost, so a pilot run underestimates a sweep. Cells whose worlds collapse to a few actors are nearly free. Cells that stay crowded (a monoculture, a growing pile of dead matter, a large reach) dominate the tail, and a short pilot reads the cheap early state before that pile builds up. In one project, a 4-run pilot missed a sweep's cost by two orders of magnitude. Another pilot missed by 3–10 times because its few hundred early ticks ran on a small pile, and another by more than ten times because it never ran the largest setting. So size a sweep from its worst cells: pilot the most expensive setting (the largest reach, the densest start) long enough to reach its settled state, and scale by more than the tick count. A brief for a sweep should name that pilot.
