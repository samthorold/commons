When research exists to replace a document written from recall, the Stance's "brief from the owner's question" has a concrete Procedure that worked: keep the recalled document out of sight of every researcher and of the report writer, and only after the report is written does the coordinator read it, add a section to the report on **where the sources disagree with the document**, and rewrite just that slice's section of the document from the report. Across nine slices every one found real disagreements: rules that had changed (a new treaty's tie-breaker, a VAT relief that only began this year), conditions recall missed, and one where recall reached the right answer for the wrong reason.

The rule has to cover the coordinator's own additions to a brief too, not only the document. One brief added a rule's rate from the coordinator's recall as context; the researcher found the rate had changed years before and had to correct it. Briefs carry the issue's questions and the owner's steers, nothing the coordinator remembers.
